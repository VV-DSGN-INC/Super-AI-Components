# Inclusive writing

Write for the person you cannot see: the one listening to the screen instead of looking at it, the
one reading a machine translation, the one who has never touched a mouse. That is a precision
practice, not a list of banned words; every rule here makes a sentence mean the same thing to more
people. [Voice & principles](?path=/docs/content-voice-principles--docs) already requires plain
words and short sentences. This page asks a narrower question about the same words: which one, for a
reader who cannot see the layout, cannot hear the idiom, or cannot click. Counted on 2026-09-04.

## Any person, no pronoun

The registry addresses the reader as _you_ and names everyone else by role: "Projects you pin",
"you have 120", "the provider answered", "Requester". No gendered pronoun appears in any registry
string, and "the user" appears in none either. Where a third person needs a pronoun at all it is
_they_, a rule going forward since no singular _they_ ships yet.

## Verbs the reader's device answers to

<DoDont
  doExample={<p className="text-sm">Drag and drop, or choose a file.</p>}
  doCaption="a gesture as one route, a universal verb as the other. Written for this page."
  dontExample={<p className="text-sm">Drag and drop, or click to browse</p>}
  dontCaption="generation-panel's shipped string: someone driving this by keyboard, switch or voice has no click."
/>

Two shipped strings name a device: `generation-panel`'s "Drag and drop, or click to browse" and
`home-shell`'s "Projects you pin show up here for one-click access." Both are gaps, not precedents.
_Select_ and _choose_ cover every input; a gesture is named only as one path among several.

Position words fail the same way, since a zoomed viewport, a phone and a screen reader's linear read
all put "below" somewhere else. `auth-shell`'s "let people continue with email below" is the shipped
instance. Point by name: "in Connections", "on the Library tab", the way `env-status` writes "replace
the key for this provider in Connections".

## What a name says

  <div className="flex w-56 flex-col gap-2">

      <Copy />
      <span className="sr-only">Copy seed</span>
    </Button>
    <p className="text-muted-foreground text-xs">a control: the verb and the thing it acts on</p>
  </div>
  <div className="flex w-64 flex-col gap-2"><EntityRow icon={<FileText aria-hidden className="size-4" />} title="quarterly-review.pdf" description="Attached to this thread" /><p className="text-muted-foreground text-xs">a glyph beside its label: decoration, aria-hidden, and the file name is the name</p></div>
</Example>

A control's name is the verb and its object, and per-row controls carry the row's identity:
`filter-bar` writes "Remove {label} filter", `asset-library` "Actions for {name}" and "Select
{name}", `generation-queue` "Cancel {title}" and "Retry {title}", `credits-indicator` "{label} —
manage plan". The category the registry still gets wrong is the constant name on a repeated control:
`stat-readout`'s "Copy", `autonomy-selector`'s "Revoke", `reset-affordance`'s "Reset", `filter-bar`'s
generic fallback when a chip's child is not a string, and `preview-tile`'s nameless frame button
below an overlay label. A list of five reads "Reset, Reset, Reset", and no gate can see it.

Name a control once. A button carrying both an `aria-label` and an `sr-only` span reads only the
label; the second name is dead text. And an `sr-only` suffix fuses with the visible text into one
word, so the hidden span carries the complete phrase or the control carries an outright `aria-label`.

<Example className="w-full max-w-sm">
  <QuotaMeter resources={[{ label: "Video minutes", used: 6, limit: 30, unit: "min", resetsIn: "Resets in 12 days" }]} /></Example>

A meter's name is what it measures and its value is a sentence: `quota-meter`'s bar is labelled by
its row and reads "6 of 30 min used" through `aria-valuetext`, so a listener gets the fact the
sighted reader gets from the fill. The unit is spelled the way the label spells it.

## What an image says

Eight images in the registry take `alt=""`, because they are decoration beside a label that already
says the thing. Thumbnails take the file name (`alt={file.name}`); a caller-supplied thumbnail takes
the caller's words (`alt={thumbnail.alt}`), and the component leaves that prop optional, so a
meaningful picture ships nameless unless the caller writes them. `empty-state` wraps its mark
`aria-hidden` for the same reason: an illustration that renders words would leak them into the name
of whatever encloses it.

## Words that travel

The idiom-class words (sanity, crazy, insane, grandfathered, blacklist, whitelist, dummy) appear
zero times in registry strings, and the figures of speech that translate badly ("end to end", "kick
off", "reach out") appear zero times too. Nothing to fix, and nothing to start. Units are spelled as
the label spells them ("min", "MB"), and the settled words in
[Writing mechanics](?path=/docs/content-writing-mechanics--docs) are terms of art a translator
renders deliberately: credits, thread, workspace, artifact.

## What the words say, by slot

- **The reader**: _you_. Never "the user".
- **A third person**: the role, and _they_ where a pronoun is unavoidable.
- **An action in body copy**: a verb no device owns, _select_ or _choose_.
- **A place in the flow**: its name, never its position.
- **An icon-only control**: the verb and its object, with the row's identity when it repeats.
- **A control that reports rather than acts**: the verdict or the measure, "Helpful", "Model
  context usage".
- **A meter**: what it measures, and a value sentence in `aria-valuetext`.
- **A decorative image or glyph**: `alt=""` or `aria-hidden`, written explicitly.
- **A meaningful image**: what it shows, in the sentence's terms; a thumbnail, the file name.

## Rules

- Address the reader as _you_; name a third person by role, _they_ where unavoidable. No gendered
  pronoun and no "the user" ship today; keep it that way.
- Verbs name no device: _select_ and _choose_, never _click_ or _tap_. The two shipped "click"s are
  gaps.
- Point by name, never by position. `auth-shell`'s "below" is the gap.
- An icon-only control's name is the verb and its object, and a repeated control carries the row's
  identity. Five components still ship a constant name.
- Name a control once, and never let an `sr-only` suffix fuse with visible text.
- A meter is labelled by what it measures and speaks its value as a sentence.
- Every image gets an `alt`: `""` for decoration, the subject or file name for meaning. A
  component that leaves `alt` to the caller documents that in its pitfalls.
- Idioms and device-bound figures of speech stay at zero.
