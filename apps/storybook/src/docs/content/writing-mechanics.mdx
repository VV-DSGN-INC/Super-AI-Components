# Writing mechanics

Mechanics are the invisible half of voice: nobody finishes a screen thinking "good capitalisation",
and the reader only notices when it wobbles. [Voice & principles](?path=/docs/content-voice-principles--docs)
decides how a line _sounds_; this page decides how it is _spelled_, from what the registry ships.
Counted on 2026-09-04, and the two wobbles the count found are named as defects rather than
precedents.

## Case

<Example className="w-full max-w-sm">
  <SectionHeader title="Recent projects" count={3} />
</Example>

Sentence case everywhere a person reads words: titles, labels, buttons, tabs, menu items, table
headers, chips. "Recent projects", "Images per run", "Output resolution", "Generation settings",
"Search assets", "New folder", "Select all" are all shipped strings, and a search of the registry's
JSX text for two capitalised words in a row returns nothing. Capitals are for proper nouns and
product names ("Claude Opus 4.5", "Northwind").

Uppercase is a style, never a spelling. Eight sites apply `uppercase` in CSS, all of them micro-labels
with `tracking-wide`, and none of them types the capitals into the string. Title Case appears only in
the AI Elements and shadcn layers, as inheritance rather than house style.

## Punctuation

The test is what the line does, not whether it parses as a sentence. A line that makes its own
statement closes with a period, even as a fragment: "Describe a shot and every result lands here.",
"Fixing the seed reproduces the same result across runs with identical settings.", "Resets in 12
days" scoping a bar does not. A title, a button, a tab, a chip or a menu item never ends in a period;
one that asks keeps its question mark ("Was this helpful?", "What went wrong?").

One mark per job, and the registry is consistent about which:

- The **interpunct** joins two facts of equal weight, 328 times: "Image · 1:1", "Audio · 0:32",
  "Text · 240 words", "Image generations · 50 of 50 used today".
- A **spaced em dash** sets off a consequence or an aside, 20 times: "Saved — this runs as-is once
  you have the credits", "Capacity is back — you can send again."
- An **unspaced en dash** marks a numeric range, 22 times: "Lines 40–58".
- The **ellipsis** is one character, `…`, on every running state: "Generating…", "Estimating
  cost…", 36 sites. One string still spells it as three periods, `skill-menu`'s
  "Search skills...", and it is the defect, not the pattern.

## Numbers and dates

<Example className="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
  <CostChip amount={17} />
  <CostChip amount={900} unit="credits/min" />
  <span className="text-muted-foreground text-sm tabular-nums">Edited 19 hours ago</span>
</Example>

A quantity the reader counts or acts on is a numeral however small: "4 credits", "3 open items",
"6 of 30 min used", "50 of 50". A number that belongs to a phrase rather than to a count stays a
word. Values that sit in a column carry `tabular-nums` (50 sites) so digits keep their width.

Relative time ships, and it ships consistently: "Edited 19 hours ago", "Checked 2 minutes ago",
"Last saved 5 days ago", "Resets in 3 days", "Last run 4 min ago". The registry never computes it;
every one of those is a finished sentence the host passes in (`savedLabel`, `checkedAt`,
`editedAgo`, `resetsIn`), which is what keeps the wording under this page's control rather than a
formatter's. Absolute dates spell the month: "March 2026", and `date-section` groups by the buckets
"Today", "Yesterday", then the month.

A chord is written modifier-first in every locale, one `Kbd` per key. `kbd`'s group is a plain
inline row, which means it reverses under `dir="rtl"` and paints a different instruction that still
looks correct; `CONTINUE.md` §8 records the `dir="ltr"` pin the primitive still needs.

## Truncation

Labels truncate; states and counts never do. `truncate` carries 65 sites and `line-clamp` only 3,
because `line-clamp-N` compiles to `display: -webkit-box` and dies against any `flex` on the same
element, while `truncate` composes with anything. A count or a chip beside a truncating label takes
`shrink-0`, so "Generation failed" survives whole at any width and the filename gives way instead.

## The words already settled

- **credits**: the unit of the Cost contract, on the chip, the line and the ring. Never "tokens" for
  spend, never "points".
- **generate · run · render**: the verb the composer performs, the thing it starts, and the result
  it produces for visual media. "Generating…" while it runs, "Generation failed" when it does not.
- **thread**: a conversation in the sidebar; **task**: a thread that is running.
- **workspace**: the container a person works in; **team** is people, never a container.
- **asset · artifact**: a generated or uploaded file in the library, and a document a conversation
  produced. `artifact-grid` and `asset-library` keep them apart.
- **model · preset · skill · source**: what generates, a saved configuration, an agent capability,
  and a cited document.
- **the six generation states**: `idle · queued · streaming · done · failed · locked`, from the
  `cost` contract, and their visible labels.
- **the four approval verbs**: Confirm, Edit, Regenerate, Skip, always in that order.
- **the cost sentence**: "Need X, you have Y", the shape `run-button` and `paywall-message` share.

## Rules

- Sentence case for every label a person reads. Capitals are proper nouns and product names.
- Uppercase is applied in CSS on a micro-label, never typed into a string.
- No terminal period on a title, button, tab, chip or menu item; a question keeps its mark. A line
  that makes its own statement closes with a period, even as a fragment.
- Interpunct for two equal facts, spaced em dash for a consequence, unspaced en dash for a range,
  and the one-character ellipsis on every running state. Three periods is a bug.
- A quantity the reader acts on is a numeral. Aligned values carry `tabular-nums`.
- Relative time is a finished sentence the host passes in; the registry never formats it.
- Labels truncate; numbers and states do not, and they take `shrink-0` beside a label that does.
- Chords are modifier-first, one key per `Kbd`.
- Reuse the settled words rather than coining a synonym.
