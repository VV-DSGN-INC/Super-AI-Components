# Voice & principles

Plain words, short sentences, no cheer. The interface is a competent colleague: it says what
happened and what to do, then stops. That voice is not aspirational. Every principle below is quoted
from copy the registry already ships, with the component named, and the one place the register slips
is named too. Two rules are gates: CPY-1 fails the build on a generic call to action, and CPY-2
warns on the marketing register.

## Front-load the verdict

<Example className="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
  <RunButton state="estimating" />
  <RunButton state="idle" cost={4} onRun={() => {}} />
</Example>

`run-button` announces "Estimating cost…" and "Generating…" in a status, and `result-card` labels its
states "Generation failed", "Result ready", "Not started". `env-status` opens every sentence with the
state word: "Key invalid. The provider answered and rejected this request's credentials." A reader
who stops after the first word still has the verdict; the clause after the period is the why.

## Verbs over nouns

<Example>
  <SuggestionChips>
    <SuggestionChip suggestion="Summarize this document" onSelect={() => {}} />
    <SuggestionChip suggestion="Find action items" onSelect={() => {}} />
    <SuggestionChip suggestion="Draft a reply" onSelect={() => {}} />
  </SuggestionChips>
</Example>

Name an action by what it does to what: verb, then object. The starter chips above are
`suggestion-chips`' own story; `run-button` says "Add credits" and "Upgrade to run";
`permission-prompt` offers "Allow once", "Always allow", "Edit first"; `approval-card` carries the
four approval verbs in the same order every time. The gate behind this is CPY-1: "Get Started" and
"Learn More" fail the build, because a label predicts the next screen and those two predict nothing.

## Say the number

<Example className="w-full max-w-lg">
  <RateLimitBanner cause="your-limit" resource="Image generations · 50 of 50 used today" remainingSeconds={1847} /></Example>

A count says how much is left; a hedge does not. `paywall-message` says "Need 900 credits, you have
120.", `quota-meter` reads "6 of 30 min used" to a screen reader, `credits-indicator` shows the
balance and `rate-limit-banner` the wait. The Cost contract makes this structural: cost is disclosed
before commit as a number beside the control, "Need 4 credits, you have 2", never as "insufficient".

## No cheerleading: outcomes speak

<Example className="w-full max-w-lg">
  <EmptyState size="panel" icon={<ImagePlus />} title={<span className="text-lg font-medium tracking-tight">No renders yet</span>} description="Describe a shot and every result lands here." action={
      <Button>
        <Sparkles aria-hidden />Generate a render</Button>
    } /></Example>

`result-card`'s "Result ready" is the success state: a fact, no adjective. `approval-card` reports
"Sent back for editing"; `paywall-message` says "The run was not started, so nothing was charged."
One string in the registry congratulates the reader: `feedback`'s default `submittedLabel`, "Thanks
for the feedback!", carries the registry's only exclamation mark in user-facing copy. The anti-slop
rules treat an exclamation mark in microcopy as a failure, so that default is a gap to close, not a
precedent.

<DoDont
  doExample={<p className="text-sm">Result ready</p>}
  doCaption="the event, stated. result-card ships this string verbatim."
  dontExample={<p className="text-sm">Nice work! Your render is on its way.</p>}
  dontCaption="praise nobody asked for plus an exclamation mark; the reader learns nothing new."
/>

## Calm about problems

Errors are calm because the reader already is not. `rate-limit-banner` says "This is the cap on your
plan, not a problem with your request." and, for the provider case, "Demand is high right now.
Nothing is wrong with your account or your request." `safety-block` says "This request wasn't sent."
and names the policy. `connection-manager` tells a rejected key what to do and why retrying will not
help. [Error messages](?path=/docs/content-error-messages--docs) owns the formula.

## No marketing register inside a workflow

CPY-2 warns on "elevate", "unlock", "empower", "supercharge", "seamless", "effortless" and their
kin. It is a warning rather than a blocker for one reason: the paywall family uses "unlock" as the
literal mechanism ("Upgrade to unlock it"), and a gate that failed the plain description of gating
would be wrong. Everywhere else the word is filler that fakes confidence a description would earn.
Feature copy belongs to the Marketing tier, which has its own components for it.

## Which voice, by situation

- **A completed action**: a neutral past-tense or state phrase, "Result ready", "Sent back to
  regenerate".
- **A running action**: the present participle with an ellipsis, in a status: "Generating…",
  "Estimating cost…".
- **A destructive confirmation**: consequence first, then buttons that name their outcomes.
  `permission-prompt` shows the action, the reason and the arguments before "Allow once".
- **A status label**: the fact, never a judgment on the reader: "Degraded", "Key invalid", "Not
  running".
- **A gate**: what it costs and what it buys, with a real path beside it: "Need 900 credits, you
  have 120." beside "Upgrade for more credits".
- **A primary action**: verb plus object: "Generate a render", "Browse presets", "New folder".
- **A tooltip or a title**: a label, no sentence, no full stop.
- **An error**: [Error messages](?path=/docs/content-error-messages--docs).
- **A zero state**: [Empty states](?path=/docs/content-empty-states--docs).

## Rules

- The verdict lands in the first word or two: "Key invalid.", "Result ready", "Generation failed".
  The why is a second sentence.
- Name an action verb-then-object. Generic wayfinding labels fail the build (CPY-1).
- Say the number wherever one exists: credits, counts, minutes, waits. Never "some", never
  "insufficient".
- No praise, no exclamation marks. The one in `feedback` is a gap.
- A status label states the fact and never grades the reader.
- A destructive or costly confirmation puts the consequence before the choice, and each button names
  its own outcome.
- No marketing register in product copy (CPY-2). "Unlock" is allowed only as the literal gating
  mechanism.
- Reuse a shipped default label before inventing one; the shipped strings are the register.
